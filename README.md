# LUNE Aerospace

Engineering the systems that extend human capability beyond Earth.

LUNE is an Africa-rooted aerospace initiative focused on the research, design, development, and eventual manufacture of space systems. Its long-term ambition is to contribute to the infrastructure, technical capability, and industrial base required for Africa to participate meaningfully in the space economy.

We approach aerospace as a systems-engineering discipline: hardware, software, electronics, materials, computation, and human institutions must work together to produce reliable machines.

Our work begins with fundamentals, progresses through experimentation and verification, and aims toward increasingly capable space systems.

# Mission

To develop the engineering capability, technologies, and industrial foundations necessary to design and build increasingly sophisticated space systems from Africa.

# Engineering Focus

Our development interests include:

** Spacecraft systems — CubeSat and small-satellite architecture, subsystem integration, and mission design.
** Embedded systems and avionics — firmware, onboard computing, electrical interfaces, and fault-aware control.

** Communications and telemetry — spacecraft data handling, communication interfaces, and ground-system integration.

** Autonomous systems — sensing, estimation, control, and onboard decision-making.

** Simulation and verification — computational models, software testing, hardware testing, and system-level validation.

** Advanced engineering and manufacturing — the progressive development of local design, fabrication, assembly, and testing capabilities.

These are areas of development and research, not a claim that every capability is already operational.

# How We Engineer

We value engineering that can be inspected, tested, explained, and improved.

# Our approach emphasizes:

1. First principles — understanding the physical and mathematical foundations behind a design.
   
3. Systems thinking — treating interfaces, dependencies, constraints, and failure modes as part of the design.
   
5. Incremental development — building and testing small, understandable systems before increasing complexity.
   
7. Technical documentation — recording requirements, assumptions, design decisions, test results, and lessons learned.
   
9. Verification — distinguishing a proposed design from a simulated result, a tested prototype, and a flight-qualified system.
    
11. Long-term capability — developing knowledge and infrastructure that compound over time.

# Research and Development

LUNE's engineering work is intended to progress from foundational experiments toward integrated systems.

Early development may include embedded firmware, sensor acquisition, telemetry, simulation, electrical interfaces, and subsystem prototypes. These foundations provide a practical route toward more demanding spacecraft engineering.

We aim to document our work so that technical decisions can be traced, experiments can be reproduced, and future development can build on verified results.

# Africa and Space

Africa's participation in the space economy requires more than access to space-based services. It also requires deeper engineering capability, research institutions, manufacturing competence, and people who can design, build, test, and maintain complex systems.

LUNE's long-term ambition is to contribute to that capability through sustained engineering work and the progressive development of indigenous technology.
